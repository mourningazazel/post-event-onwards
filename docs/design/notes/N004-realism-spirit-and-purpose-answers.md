# N004 — The spirit of the game, and answers to the purpose questions

- Date: 2026-09-27
- Source: owner, in session

> You absoultely should be able to use lures or decoys with the scent mechanics. In fact, the build
> up of certain scents, should help drive away mobs and lower the build up mechanic. that way if
> you stay in one area for a long time, you have ways to combat the eventual build up of crowds.
> Yes, I believe Rain and strong winds should make travel outside much more feasible. While winds
> should make scents travel faster, it will also cause the scents to drift upwards faster. Scents
> are only recorded on ground tiles, so there is no accumulation in the sky. Vision units are
> triggered much like you listed, they would be sort of boss or hunter-type units. Rare to find,
> but a challenge not only due to their increased pathfinding mechanics, but also the fact that
> their triggered agitation would naturally draw crowds towards you. Being swarmed is a main
> mechanic on how you can die. Tripping absoultely is exploitable, as that introduces terrain as a
> gameplay mechanic for building defense, as well as a punishment mechanic for the player who also
> has to deal with tripping. It is also meant as incidental chaos to slow down crowds. A crowd of
> zombies moving over a city street would be much different than thick underbrush. The intended
> uses for digging would be what you listed for the most part, because the emphasis of the
> gameplay is that you "can" do that if you'd like. Making a hole that you can stand up in / one
> whole z level, would be a very large endeavor. But you could always use a hole for unique
> things, like a mote around your entrance, spikes at the bottom etc. (you dont need physics
> system, just an acceleration value and a z level drop type calculation). I used the "Saw through
> wall" mainly as an example, but since this is a "all goes", yes, you coudl use it to break into
> a building or you could use it to escape a building etc. But this will be based off material
> properties, and then the material properties / uses of the items you wish to do it with (or your
> bare hands if thats something you're even remotely capable of). The spirit of the game is a
> super realistic take on zombie survival, where you can do pretty much anything you can do in
> normal life, and the city / buildings will be populated just as much as they would be in normal
> life, but there would also be just as many zombies as there would be in a real outbreak (you are
> one of a very very very very select few who are not dead, and almost everyone else just turned,
> and is now walking the street).

## Guiding principle this sets for all design

**The game's spirit:** a super-realistic take on zombie survival.

- You can do almost anything you could do in real life.
- Cities and buildings are as populated, with objects, as real ones.
- There are as many zombies as a real outbreak would produce: almost everyone turned, and the
  player is one of very few survivors.

When a design choice is unclear, prefer the option that is **more physically plausible**, and let
material and tool properties decide outcomes, rather than special-case rules.
