# PEO-088 · Siege suite: the horde round three buildings for 48 game hours, measured at six checkpoints (N022)

## Goal

A separate program, peo_siege, runs the horde round each of three buildings in three opening setups at two densities for 48 game hours and writes a readable report of where the Dead are and what they are doing at 1, 2, 6, 12, 24 and 48 h. It runs on demand, only when a change could affect horde or scent mechanics, and is never part of verify, ctest or the regular CI jobs (Ryan, 2026-10-02); its --check against a committed baseline shows such a change as a reviewed diff

## Context

- Ryan's words: docs/design/notes/N022-siege-scenarios.md. Horde and scent mechanics only: when other mechanics land, they stay out of this suite
- Not covered today: tests/core/test_swarm.cpp runs the draw on open stages for a few dozen updates with a hand-rolled loop. Nothing runs World against a building for hours
- Why its own suite: 18 configurations x 48 h is 28,800 updates each, minutes of CPU. The D-033 tiers are 1 s and 5 s, and the scenario tier already uses most of its budget (PEO-086). peo_siege sits beside the tests, like the planned peo_bench (PEO-070), outside suite_budget
- World builds its stage only from generate_stage (src/core/src/world.cpp load_stage) and places the Dead at random, so it needs a way to load a hand-built stage, player and horde (unit 1). Doors are wall gaps today; there is no door object
- The Dead are all calm today (PEO-009, D-038 B): scent, company and stay terms. Calm Dead never share a tile (D-031). There is no alerted or following state, no sound and no attraction between Dead beyond company (PEO-010), no shared tiles or trample (PEO-022), no crowd pressure on walls (PEO-084), no rot (PEO-039) and no soak (PEO-038). Each will change this suite's numbers when it lands, which is the point
- Nothing stops one of the Dead stepping onto the player's tile, and nothing happens if it does. The suite counts contact; it adds no contact rule
- Walls stop the geodesic field (D-024), so with no opening no scent leaves the building and the outside Dead only wander and gather by company. That run is the control the others are read against
- Layout, every building (interior sizes are floor cells; walls are one cell thick round every floor and hallway cell; 8-connected moves, no corner cutting, PEO-044). Stage 161 x 161 with its border wall, the building centred, every other cell open and outdoors; the building's footprint (walls included) is indoors (PEO-048 openness). Wind calm (wind_max 0)
- Building A, one room: 5 x 5 floor
- Building B, two rooms: two 4 x 4 floors side by side, west and east. A hallway 1 cell wide and 4 cells long runs east from a gap in the west room's east wall to a gap in the east room's west wall, on floor row 2 (rows 0 to 3 from the north). The two wall gaps are doorways, not hallway cells
- Building C, two rooms and a bent hallway: as B, but the hallway is 9 cells: 3 east, then 3 south, then 3 east (both 90-degree turns are inside the hallway), so the east room sits 3 rows lower than the west
- Opening setups. Enclosed: no gaps in the outer walls. One opening: a 1-cell gap in the west room's west wall on floor row 2 (A: the middle row of 5), and the player on the east room's easternmost floor cell on row 2 (A: the east column, middle row), the far end from the opening. Two openings: the same plus a mirror gap in the east room's east wall on row 2; the player stays where the one-opening setup puts them (one change from that test), so in this setup they stand beside the east opening. Enclosed puts the player where one opening does
- Densities: sparse = 5% of the outdoor open cells, heavy = 40%, placed uniformly from the run's seed, never inside the footprint; speeds drawn as load_stage draws them (1 to 3 updates a step). The player waits the whole run in 6 s waits (Action::wait)
- Matrix: 3 buildings x 3 setups x 2 densities = 18 configurations, each over 3 seeds, one building on its own stage per run
- Ryan, 2026-10-02: run the siege suite only when a change could affect those mechanics, not in the regular testing; the unit and scenario tiers already cover the basic mechanics

## Units

- core: a way to load a hand-built stage into World, for example World::load_layout(Stage, Vec2i player, horde positions and step_seconds): the same reset as load_stage (scent, wind from the stage's openness and the seed, slots, desire field, clock) without generate_stage or the random spawn, and no exit, so the stage never advances. Doctests: a loaded layout keeps every given position and the player; it never advances on its own; commit(speculate()) stays bit-identical to step() on a loaded layout; calm Dead on it never share a tile
- siege: tests/siege/ (or tools/siege/, your call) with the three buildings, three setups and two densities as data, built from the Context's rules, and a fixture test that prints each building as ASCII and checks floor and hallway cell counts (A 25; B 32 + 4; C 32 + 9), that the enclosed footprint has no route from outside to the player, that each opening gives one, and that the player is the far cell the setup names
- siege: the runner, peo_siege. Runs the matrix (or a filter: --building, --setup, --density, --seeds), each run on its own World, runs side by side on a thread pool (src/app/thread_pool.hpp is header-only) since they share nothing. At each checkpoint it measures the run (below) and keeps the seed means with min and max
- Measurements at each checkpoint, per configuration: (1) inside: Dead on floor cells by room and on hallway cells, and on the opening cells; (2) placement spread: Dead by Chebyshev distance from the footprint in bands 1 (touching an outer wall), 2-3, 4-10, 11-30, 31-60, 61+, with the mean and standard deviation of that distance; (3) at the openings: Dead within 3 cells outside each; (4) by side: Dead within 10 cells of the footprint west, east, north and south of it; (5) player: Dead on the player's tile and on the 8 round it; (6) behaviour: share of the horde that moved since the last checkpoint, mean distance from its start, share standing on scent (sample > 0), and a count per behaviour state read from core (one column, calm, today; a state core gains later appears without changing the runner)
- Per configuration also: the game time the first Dead stands inside, and the first reaches the player's tile or touches it (or 'never')
- Report: a markdown file, one section per building, one table per setup and density with a row per checkpoint, plus a short first section listing the 'firsts' for all 18. Maps in a second file: for seed 0 of every configuration, at every checkpoint, the 41 x 41 cells round the building as text (# wall, . floor or ground, d one of the Dead, @ the player, + both), so spread can be seen as well as counted
- Baseline: commit the two files under tests/siege/baseline/. peo_siege --check compares a fresh run to them and prints the first differing lines; --write replaces them. A change to horde or scent mechanics updates the baseline in the same commit, and the commit message says why the numbers moved
- peo_siege is compiled by the normal build so it never rots, but no ctest, verify stage or regular CI job runs it. Its fixture test (unit 2) is the only part in the regular suite
- ci: a separate siege workflow triggered only by workflow_dispatch (manual), on headless-release: builds peo_siege, runs --check over the full matrix and uploads the fresh report as an artifact either way
- docs: a siege row in docs/testing.md's layers table saying it is on demand, with the rule for when to run it: a change to the Dead's movement, draw or knobs, the scent field or wind, the desire field, or a new horde mechanic (PEO-010, 022, 038, 039, 051, 084); such a change runs peo_siege --check and, if it differs, commits the new baseline with the reason. One line in .agents/skills/plan/SKILL.md so briefs for those changes list it under Tests, and a line under 'Reproducible swarm tests' in docs/design/scent-mobs.md pointing to it

## Acceptance

- peo_siege runs all 18 configurations x 3 seeds for 48 game hours and writes the report and the maps; report the wall time on the M1 (release, all cores) and in the manual CI run. Budget: the CI run under 10 minutes. If it is over, drop to 2 seeds first, then a 141 x 141 stage, and say which
- Invariants, checked at every checkpoint of every run and failing the program: never two Dead on one tile; the horde's size never changes; with the enclosed setup no Dead is ever inside the footprint and no scent cell outside it is above 0
- Deterministic: the report is byte-identical serial and with threads, and across runs; --check passes on the committed baseline on the Builder's arm64 and in one manual CI run on x86 (the Architect can trigger it)
- Ryan can read it: every table has a header saying what its units are, times are game hours, and the firsts section reads as plain sentences or a table; paste the firsts section and the 48 h rows for building C, two openings, both densities, into the report note
- verify.py, ctest and the regular CI jobs do not run peo_siege; their times do not change beyond the fixture test
- python3 tools/verify.py --full passes

- A moving player, wind, doors as objects, other building shapes: later variations, by queue item
- Putting peo_siege in the D-033 tiers or changing their budgets
